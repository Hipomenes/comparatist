Dear students,

We are now in the last stretch of the spring quarter, and it feels like a fitting moment to pause and take stock. This inaugural issue of The Comparatist looks back on the undergraduate conference and the “Comp Lit Goes to Grad School” round table, celebrates three graduating seniors heading into their next chapter, gathers a few current reading recommendations from across our community, and invites you into an ongoing conversation about AI and the humanities.

Going forward, a new issue of The Comparatist will arrive in your inbox each quarter, with news from the department, opportunities to keep on your radar, and space for your own voices.

 

Reading with Theory: A Recap

On April 18, we hosted the second annual UCI Comparative Literature Undergraduate Conference in Critical Theory, with sixteen presenters from across the UC system, the CSUs, and as far away as Florida International University. Four panels — Time, The Body, Technology, and Ideology — generated genuinely sharp conversation across the day.

We are particularly proud of our own Comp Lit majors who presented:

  • Sabella Habtemariam — “Reinterpreting Medea through Anti-Productivity”
  • Elizabeth Lieb — “Mothers: How Changes in Parenting Created More Artists”
  • Benjamin Li — “Symptomatic Readings of Lu Xun from Jameson and Althusser to Lyotard”
  • Alinur Isakov — “Fascism, Finitude, Failure in Little Malcolm and His Struggle Against the Eunuchs”

Congratulations to all four — and thanks to everyone who came out to listen, ask questions, and stay for the reception.

 

Comp Lit Goes to Grad School

On Monday, April 27, we gathered in HIB 246 for a round-table conversation with current graduate students Madison Noelle Mackenzie and Jacob Reimer, recent alumna Valentina Disacco, and graduating senior Ria Singh Thakur, who heads to UCSB Comparative Literature this fall. Together they offered candid perspectives on the path from undergraduate study to graduate school — choosing programs, preparing application materials (personal statements, writing samples, letters of recommendation), navigating funding and fellowships, and what day-to-day graduate life actually looks like. Many thanks to our four speakers for their generosity, and to everyone who came with questions.

 

Student Spotlights

This quarter we celebrate three graduating seniors and welcome one incoming major.

Incoming Major

Meredith Lo

Meredith is a programmer with an affinity for poetry, interested in how narratives can be crafted through both the literal and visual significance of words. She is currently studying Computer Science at UC Irvine and intends to pursue research at the intersection of technology and the humanities.

Graduating Senior

Ria Singh Thakur

Ria came to UCI's Comparative Literature program as a transfer student from Irvine Valley College. Previously a Physics major, she decided to pursue a degree in Comparative Literature after being drawn to its interdisciplinary, multilingual, and experimental rigor. As an undergraduate, she took advantage of UCI's course offerings in foreign languages, building on a background in Hindi, Telugu, and Korean with the study of Ancient Greek and German, and extended this formal training through her own research — focusing primarily on Greek tragedy, its aesthetic receptions and formal limitations, augmenting its study through scholars such as G.W.F. Hegel, Jacques Lacan, and Bertolt Brecht.

In the fall, she will be joining UC Santa Barbara's Comparative Literature program as a graduate student, where she plans to further explore her interests in tragedy, psychoanalysis, German idealism, and Marxism. She is deeply grateful to UCI's faculty for always supporting her curiosity and challenging her as she prepares for graduate study and a future in academia.

Graduating Senior

Aniela Giovanna Crescini

Aniela graduates this spring with dual degrees in Comparative Literature and Physical Sciences — and a clear sense of where she is headed. After graduation, she will be taking a purposeful gap year before entering a dual J.D./Ph.D. program combining both Law and Literature, committed to grounding legal education in real experience by working in litigation environments first.

Her background in Comparative Literature is central to this path: the close reading, critical theory, and attention to power and narrative that define her academic work translate directly into the interpretive demands of legal practice and advocacy. In the long term, she hopes to move between law and education — fighting for justice and cultivating it in the classroom. After all, the law is a text, and literature taught her how to read it, as well as what is left out.

Graduating Senior

Aliannah Shalikar

For Aliannah, studying Comparative Literature at UCI has been one of the most transformative experiences of her academic career — a program that has sharpened her critical awareness of an ever-changing world and deepened her interest in social and political reform. She hopes to continue her studies at the graduate level, pursuing a master's degree in Comparative Literature or Education, with the long-term goal of working in academia and education policy.

These are just a few of our graduating seniors and incoming majors — the list is far from exhaustive. Students are warmly encouraged to share their own bios with the editor for inclusion in upcoming newsletters: dhcolmen@uci.edu.

 

In Our Bookshelves

What members of the Comp Lit community are reading this quarter.

Recommended by David H. Colmenares, Assistant Professor

The Last Wolf & Herman · László Krasznahorkai

A master trapper hired by the municipal forest hunts back, while we are drawn into the endless world-disclosing possibilities of hypotaxis.

 

Recommended by Carmen Lin, Comp Lit Major

The Unselected Journals of Emma M. Lion, Vol. 1 · Beth Brower

Live and breathe with charmingly crosspatched Emma M. Lion in the eclectic Lapis Lazuli House in London, 1883.

 

Recommended by Sarah Hoenicke Flores, Comp Lit PhD Candidate

Living as a Bird · Vinciane Despret

Despret multiplies worlds, ways of accounting for difference, and habits of reading, asking: What changes when we consider what matters to birds?

Fall 2026 Grants

The most competitive national scholarships (Fulbright, Strauss, and others) have application cycles that open in Fall 2026, but the real work begins this summer. Strong applications may take months to develop, so now is the time to start.

If you are considering applying, you are warmly encouraged to reach out to me (dhcolmen@uci.edu) or to any of your Comparative Literature professors. We are glad to help you think through projects, programs, and fit.

Two campus resources are especially worth knowing:

  • The UCI Scholarship Opportunities Program (SOP), in 193 Science Library, handles endorsements and application preparation for undergraduates and seniors. Reach them at sklrship@uci.edu or visit scholars.uci.edu.
  • For UCI-internal funding, the department is glad to support students applying for UROP grants (and, next summer, SURP). Deadlines fall later in the year, but students typically begin developing a project with a faculty mentor over the summer. See urop.uci.edu.
